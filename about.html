My Transition

My path into software engineering is nontraditional. I built my career in enterprise risk and governance, where I learned to think in systems: understand requirements, identify failure points, communicate across teams, and design processes that hold up under real constraints.

I am now pairing that experience with graduate software engineering coursework and hands-on development. I learn best by building, testing, and explaining real applications rather than treating programming as a collection of isolated exercises.

What I’m Building

My primary project is TCH — Technology for Caregivers in the Home, a React Native application focused on caregiver coordination and care-event tracking. I designed the prototype around real mobile-use constraints such as divided attention, touch-friendly interactions, fast logging, and clear handoffs.

The next phase is turning the working prototype into a maintainable beta product: modular architecture, configurable onboarding, authentication, shared-care data, cloud synchronization, testing, and stronger security controls.

How I’m Developing

I am strengthening React Native and TypeScript while continuing Java and data structures / algorithms practice. I use GitHub to document progression and am intentionally building the engineering habits behind the code: small changes, pull requests, clear documentation, automated checks, testing, and the ability to explain design decisions.

What I Bring to an Engineering Team

  • Systems thinking: I naturally connect technical details to process and business impact.
  • Governance mindset: I think about controls, failure modes, accountability, and risk early.
  • Communication: I am comfortable translating complex topics across technical and nontechnical audiences.
  • Execution: I am used to balancing work, military responsibilities, graduate study, and hands-on development.
  • Product orientation: I care about whether software is understandable and useful, not just whether it runs.

Current Goal

I am working toward a software engineering role where I can contribute to front-end, mobile, or full-stack systems while continuing to deepen my technical foundation. My GitHub is structured to show that progression transparently—from introductory coursework to increasingly complete applications.
